What a facelift addresses

Facelift surgery generally focuses on selected laxity of the lower face and neck. It does not directly correct every concern involving eyelids, brows, skin texture or volume. The exact operation and incision pattern should follow an examination and a clear discussion of priorities.

ProcedureSurgery
ScarsUsually around ear and hairline
RecoveryIndividual and staged
ResultLong-lasting, not permanent

Different names, individual plans

Terms such as mini, deep-plane and SMAS facelift describe differing scopes or techniques, but marketing labels alone do not establish suitability. Ask the surgeon what tissue will be treated, where scars will sit and why their plan fits your anatomy.

Planning recovery

Bruising, swelling, tightness and temporary altered sensation are expected to varying degrees. Returning to social activity is not the same as complete healing. Arrange practical support, follow wound-care advice and understand when the surgeon wants to review you.

Risks and important questions

Potential complications include bleeding or haematoma, infection, poor wound healing, visible or widened scars, asymmetry, hairline change, altered sensation and injury to facial nerve branches. Smoking can increase healing risks.

  • What operation is proposed and what will it not improve?
  • Where will the scars be and how do they usually mature?
  • What is the surgeon’s specialist registration and facelift experience?
  • Who manages problems outside normal hours?
  • How could later ageing or weight change affect the result?
Compare substance, not labels. Technique names and dramatic photographs cannot replace a personal risk-benefit discussion.
