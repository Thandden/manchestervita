What liposuction can address

Liposuction removes selected deposits of subcutaneous fat through small incisions and a cannula. It may alter contour in areas such as the abdomen, flanks, thighs, arms or under the chin. It does not treat obesity, guarantee weight loss or reliably tighten significantly loose skin.

Technique names

Tumescent, power-assisted, ultrasound-assisted and other terms describe equipment or methods. The name alone does not establish a better result. More useful questions concern surgeon experience, volume planned, anaesthesia, fluid management, skin quality and how contour irregularity will be minimised.

Recovery

Swelling, bruising, soreness and fluid leakage from small incisions may occur early on. Compression garments are often advised. Final contour may take months to settle. The surgeon should explain activity restrictions, clot prevention and signs that require urgent review.

Risks and alternatives

Risks include bleeding, infection, seroma, altered sensation, contour irregularity, asymmetry, skin injury and thrombosis. Larger-volume or combined operations may carry greater risk. Loose skin may require excisional surgery such as a tummy tuck rather than—or alongside—fat removal.

  • What contour change is realistic for my skin and anatomy?
  • How much fat is planned for removal?
  • Where will surgery and recovery monitoring occur?
  • What is the surgeon’s complication and revision policy?
  • Could a smaller procedure or no surgery meet my goal?
Weight and contour are different questions. A responsible assessment should discuss stable weight, skin quality and expectations rather than sell liposuction as slimming.
