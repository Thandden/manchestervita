What resurfacing may address

Laser resurfacing may be considered for selected texture, fine-line, acne-scar or pigment concerns. It cannot remove every scar or guarantee even skin. Diagnosis is important because some pigmentation or active skin conditions may need a different approach.

Ablative, non-ablative and fractional

These terms describe how the device affects skin and whether treatment is delivered in columns. They imply different downtime and risk profiles, but they do not identify the exact laser. Ask for the device name, wavelength, settings approach and why it suits the diagnosed concern and your skin tone.

Aftercare

Redness, swelling, sensitivity, crusting or peeling vary by treatment. More intensive resurfacing requires wound care and longer recovery. Sun avoidance and high-protection sunscreen are usually important; follow the treating professional’s instructions rather than generic online routines.

Risks and questions

Possible harms include burns, infection, cold-sore reactivation, prolonged redness, scarring and lighter or darker pigmentation. Pigment change can be a particular consideration for darker skin tones and recent tanning.

  • What diagnosis and device support this treatment plan?
  • What experience does the operator have with my skin tone?
  • Is a test patch appropriate?
  • What downtime and number of sessions are realistic?
  • Who manages burns, infection or pigment change?
“Laser” is not one treatment. Judge the exact device, operator and plan—not the category name alone.
