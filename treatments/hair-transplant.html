Hair restoration guide

Hair transplant treatment in Manchester.

Hair transplantation redistributes limited donor hair. Diagnosis, long-term loss and careful hairline planning matter more than a headline graft count.

FUE and FUT

Follicular unit extraction removes small grafts individually; follicular unit transplantation removes a strip from which grafts are prepared. Both move hair rather than create new follicles. They differ in scarring, shaving, donor management and recovery, and neither method is automatically best for everyone.

Diagnosis before design

Not every form of hair loss is suitable for transplantation. Assessment should consider pattern, stability, scalp health, donor density, medicines, family history and age. A conservative hairline may age more naturally and preserve donor supply for possible future loss.

Recovery and growth

Redness, crusting, swelling and temporary numbness may occur. Transplanted hairs commonly shed before later growth begins. Density develops gradually and outcomes vary. Existing non-transplanted hair can continue to thin.

Risks and questions

Risks include bleeding, infection, scarring, poor growth, unnatural direction, overharvesting, temporary shedding and persistent numbness. Ask which named clinician diagnoses, designs, makes incisions and oversees graft handling.

  • What diagnosis explains my hair loss?
  • What donor supply is available over my lifetime?
  • Who performs each stage of the procedure?
  • What result is realistic in one session?
  • What plan addresses future hair loss?
Graft counts need context. More grafts are not automatically better if donor supply, survival and long-term design are compromised.
