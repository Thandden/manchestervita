Breast surgery guide

Breast reduction in Manchester.

Reduction surgery may change breast size, weight and position. It also creates permanent scars and can affect sensation and breastfeeding.

What surgery changes

Breast reduction removes selected skin and breast tissue, reshapes the breast and repositions the nipple. People may seek it for size-related discomfort, activity limitations, skin irritation or proportion. A consultation should separate hoped-for symptom improvement from cosmetic goals without promising either.

Size and scar planning

An exact cup size cannot be guaranteed. The surgeon should explain likely proportions, incision pattern, tissue removal and whether significant asymmetry can be improved. In some cases nipple blood supply affects the techniques available.

Recovery

Swelling, bruising and soreness are expected. Support garments and temporary lifting or exercise restrictions may be advised. Wounds where scars meet can take longer to heal. Arrange practical support and ask when work, driving and exercise are realistic.

Risks and questions

Risks include bleeding, infection, delayed healing, prominent scars, asymmetry, altered or lost nipple sensation, fat necrosis and possible revision. Breastfeeding ability may be reduced, and rarely nipple or skin tissue can be lost.

  • What scar pattern and size change are realistic?
  • How might breastfeeding and sensation be affected?
  • What factors raise my wound-healing risk?
  • How are removed tissues examined?
  • Who handles wound concerns and longer-term follow-up?
Discuss future plans. Pregnancy, weight change and breastfeeding priorities belong in the first consultation, not after surgery is booked.
